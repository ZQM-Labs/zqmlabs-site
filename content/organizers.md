## The one-hour setup

1. **Print** — 7 pages total (6 signs + 1 guide). Card stock is nice; plain
   paper taped to a stake works fine.
2. **Place** — Walk your route once and plant the six signs at the marked
   spots. Your venue, your choice of exact placement.
3. **Post the code** — Teams enter one access code to start. Post it on a
   flyer, a group chat, or hand out the printable card.
4. **Set out a candy bowl** — Optional. Historically very popular.

For an approved, delivered event kit, the configured player may run from the first QR scan; the free Wisp demo remains available separately.

## Day-of troubleshooting

**A sign is missing or damaged.** Print a replacement from your kit PDF and
re-place it. Nothing else changes — the puzzle lives at the *location*, and
teams can also reach any stop from the in-game map.

**A team can't scan the QR.** Every stop is also reachable by tapping it on
the map inside the game. No QR required.

**A team's phone died.** Progress saves on the device, but a team can start
fresh on any phone with the same access code. Team name keeps their score.

**Rain.** Florida showers pass. The window spans days — teams pick a dry
moment. Kit access still expires after the October 30–November 1, 2026 window.

**A puzzle is too hard for a young team.** Every stop has **three hints**,
revealed one at a time. Hints never block a finish.

**Something is actually broken.** Email the Lab. Limited, best-effort remote support is included through your event window; it is not a monitored phone line, and replies may be delayed on Halloween.

## What you never have to do

- Build a map, write a puzzle, or design anything
- Run a planning committee (that's the point of the mantra)
- Run a live leaderboard unless an agreed package includes a tested scoring feature
- Collect or publish scores without an agreed, consent-aware event workflow
- Staff the route — the game is self-guided and staggered by design

## Support window
