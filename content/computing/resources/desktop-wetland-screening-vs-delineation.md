A preliminary **desktop wetland screening** uses available maps and datasets to flag questions for follow-up. A **field wetland delineation** evaluates site conditions under applicable methods. A **formal jurisdictional or agency determination** is a distinct regulatory process. These activities should not be described interchangeably.

## What each stage can establish

| Task | Typical evidence | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop indicator review | National Wetlands Inventory polygons, available soils, imagery and local mapping | Actual on-site wetland boundary, absence of wetlands, permit eligibility |
| Field delineation | Site observations and applicable technical methodology by appropriately qualified personnel | Automatic approval by every agency |
| Agency determination / permit review | Jurisdiction-specific process, application and agency decision | A result guaranteed by earlier mapping |

The [USFWS National Wetlands Inventory FAQ](https://www.fws.gov/page/national-wetlands-inventory-frequently-asked-questions) states that mapped wetland extent is not a jurisdictional determination. [Florida DEP's wetland evaluation and delineation program](https://floridadep.gov/water/submerged-lands-environmental-resources-coordination/content/wetland-evaluation-and) describes the state's methodology and formal-determination role. Applicable requirements depend on location and regulatory program.

## Why a blank map is not a cleared parcel

A GIS report should distinguish at least four situations:

1. **Mapped indicator intersects the site** — record the published feature, classification, date and source, then identify questions for review.
2. **No indicator intersects and coverage is verified** — report no mapped intersection *within that dataset's documented coverage and limitations*.
3. **Coverage is missing or unknown** — label the finding `unknown` and avoid a negative determination.
4. **Sources disagree** — display or summarize conflicting evidence, source dates and questions for field/agency follow-up.

Wetlands may have been mapped from imagery of a different date or at a different scale. The USFWS cautions that landscape changes and unmapped habitat types affect interpretation; see its [data limitations and precautions](https://www.fws.gov/page/wetlands-data-limitations-exclusions-and-precautions).

## A defensible preliminary-screening deliverable

For a clearly identified parcel or project boundary, a desktop GIS production scope can include:

- An aerial context map and a separate wetland-indicator map with readable legends.
- Dataset names, access dates, mapped vintage and coverage/uncertainty notes.
- An intersection summary that distinguishes `mapped`, `not_mapped_with_coverage` and `unknown`.
- A short source register and review questions, including whether field evaluation or agency consultation is needed.

**Do not infer “wetland-free,” “buildable,” “permittable” or “jurisdictionally cleared” from this desktop output.** ZQM Computing's preliminary screening service does not include field delineation, legal or engineering opinions, Phase I ESA or regulatory signoff.
