GooseChase is good at what it does. If you want to author your own scavenger hunt — write the missions, set the points, judge the photos — it is the right tool, and this page will not pretend otherwise. The question is whether authoring is what you actually want to do.

There are two different jobs here, and they suit two different organizers:

- **Build a hunt.** You write missions, test them, and run the event. GooseChase is built for this.
- **Play a quest.** You gather your group and play a finished story. ZQM Labs is built for this.

If you are here because you started building in GooseChase and realized the building is the whole job, read on. If you love building, stop here — GooseChase is your answer, and you should use it.

## GooseChase, plainly

GooseChase is a builder platform. You create an Experience from missions — photo challenges, video submissions, GPS check-ins, text answers — and players complete them in the app or, since August 2026, as guests in a web browser. You can start from a template or from scratch, and there is a fully managed path if you want their team to build it for you.

**What it costs (checked 2026-11-11 on goosechase.com):** free to build and run an Experience for up to 3 participants. A single Experience starts at $99, one-time, for up to 10 participants. Larger single experiences and subscriptions for multiple experiences exist; those prices are not published on their site. K-12 educators get their own plans: Educator Basic is free for up to 5 teams, Educator Plus is $99 per educator per year.

**What you do:** everything. You write every mission, set the scoring, test the flow, and run the event. The platform gives you the tools; the hunt itself is yours to author. That is the point of it — and it is also the work.

## ZQM Labs, plainly

ZQM Labs does the authoring for you. Dr. Z, the Lab's mad-scientist Game Master, builds each quest around a story — six stops, a puzzle at each stop, three hints per stop, and four comfort levels (Li'l Z Cozy, Classic Z Spooky, Mad Z After Dark, Full Madness Overdrive) so the same quest fits children, families, and adults after dark. Wisp, his escaped first creation, is the one leading players through it. Your group gathers, one phone per team, and plays it in a browser. No app to install.

**What it costs:** published flat fees, one-time — Family Night $49, Community $199, Office Team $349, Office Event $499. No per-player charge, no subscription. One fee for the whole group, whatever its size.

**What you do:** almost nothing before the day. Pick a kit, gather your team, walk the route, solve the puzzles. A one-page organizer guide covers setup, which takes about an hour.

## The price, worked out

For a group of 10 playing once: GooseChase starts at $99 for a single Experience. A ZQM Labs Community kit is $199 flat — more than GooseChase's entry tier, but the Lab's kit is a finished six-stop story with puzzles, hints, and four comfort levels already written, and it covers an unlimited-size community group rather than 10 participants. A Family Night kit is $49 flat for one household — less than GooseChase's $99 entry tier.

For a group that plays once and never builds again, the finished quest is usually the cheaper hour: the building time is the cost GooseChase does not put on its pricing page. For a group that builds once and runs the same hunt ten times, GooseChase wins — you pay for the Experience and reuse your own work.
