## Four formats, one idea

Every location-based game sends players outside with a phone and a goal. What changes is who the game is for, what you actually do, and how much of it someone built for you. Here are the four formats, as they are.

### Location quest

A story-driven scavenger hunt built around a place — your venue, your neighborhood, your town. A team follows a trail of stops, solves a puzzle at each one, and finishes together. A ZQM Labs quest has six stops, four comfort levels (Cozy, Spooky, After Dark, Overdrive) so the same quest fits the whole group, and one phone per team.

- **Players:** teams of four to eight, one phone per team. Built for groups.
- **Where:** your venue or a coordinated local setting — not a fixed route someone else chose.
- **Cost:** one flat fee (Family Night $49, Community $199, Office Team $349, Office Event $499). No per-player charges.
- **Setup:** about an hour with the organizer kit, after the route and permissions are agreed with the Lab.
- **Best for:** a group that wants a shared outdoor event with a story — a family night, a community fundraiser, a team offsite.

### City game

An app- or browser-guided game in a city: follow a route, solve puzzles at landmarks, finish at a set point. The route is fixed — the same route for every group that buys it.

- **Players:** usually a small team, sometimes solo.
- **Where:** a city the game already covers. You play where the route is, not where you are.
- **Cost:** usually per team or per route.
- **Setup:** none — the route already exists.
- **Best for:** a visiting team that wants a quick, no-planning activity in a city they do not know.

### Outdoor escape game

Escape-room puzzles taken outside: a story and a set of puzzles spread across a park or a neighborhood, usually timed, usually solved by one team working together.

- **Players:** one small team at a time.
- **Where:** a park or neighborhood the operator uses.
- **Cost:** usually per team or per person.
- **Setup:** none for the players; the operator sets the puzzles.
- **Best for:** puzzle lovers who want an escape-room feel with fresh air — and a team small enough to crowd around one puzzle at a time.

### GPS treasure hunt

The best-known format is geocaching: players use an app to find hidden containers left by other players, all over the world. The hunt itself is the game — find the cache, sign the log, move on.

- **Players:** solo players and small groups. Built for the individual hunter.
- **Where:** anywhere caches exist — which is almost everywhere.
- **Cost:** free to start, with a paid tier for the full map.
- **Setup:** none — download the app and go.
- **Best for:** someone who loves exploring and collecting finds on their own schedule, one cache at a time.

## How they differ

| Feature | Location quest | City game | Outdoor escape game | GPS treasure hunt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Built for | Groups and teams | Small teams, visitors | One small team | Solo players |
| Story | Yes — written for the event | Light, route-based | Yes — one story | No — the hunt is the game |
| Venue | Your venue, coordinated | Fixed city route | Operator's park or area | Anywhere caches exist |
| Device | Browser, one phone per team | App or browser | Usually an app | App |
| Cost shape | Flat fee per event | Per team or route | Per team or person | Free to start |
| Setup | About an hour + coordination | None | None for players | None |

## Choosing by group and occasion

- **A family night or a birthday:** a location quest. The comfort levels let children and adults play the same quest, and the flat Family Night fee ($49, or $25 with FAMILY25) covers the household.
- **A visiting team with no time to plan:** a city game. Show up, play the route, done.
- **Puzzle lovers, one team, an afternoon:** an outdoor escape game. It is the closest thing to an escape room with sky above it.
- **A solo player who likes exploring:** a GPS treasure hunt. Nothing else in this list is built for one person wandering with a phone.
- **A team offsite or a community fundraiser:** a location quest. Flat pricing holds up at group size, and the story gives the day a shape a task list does not. See the [team-building guide](/guides/team-building-games/) for how to choose by group size.
- **A school or youth group:** a location quest, coordinated with the Lab. The [youth-group guide](/guides/youth-group-scavenger-hunt/) covers supervision, safety, and group sizes.
