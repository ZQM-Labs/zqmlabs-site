## Running a quest for a youth group

A youth-group quest is the same game as a family quest, with the supervision and safety planned first. The game itself is simple: teams of four to eight, one phone per team, six stops, a puzzle at each one, three hints if a team gets stuck. The planning around it is where a youth leader earns the day.

**Group size.** Teams of four to eight work best. A group of 20 splits into three or four teams; a group of 40 splits into six or seven. One adult per team is the plan — the adult walks with the team, holds the phone if the players are young, and keeps the pace.

**Adult supervision.** Young teams need an adult with them. Say so in your sign-up, and size your volunteer plan around it. The adult is not a player — they are the team's guide, timekeeper, and safety net.

**Safety.** Walk the route yourself first, at the same time of day your group will play. Check road crossings, shade, water, and phone signal. Tell every team where the finish is and what to do if they get separated. Keep a charged phone on the adult, not just on the players.

**Accessibility.** Not every young person walks the same way. Check your route for step-free access, shade, seating, and distance before you announce it, and say plainly what the route asks of players. Tell the Lab about any accessibility needs when you coordinate — the route is agreed for your group, not imposed on it.

**Weather.** Florida showers pass. A multi-day event window lets teams pick a dry moment, and the Lab will extend a window if a real storm wipes out the weekend — ask, don't assume. Have a rain date in mind before you announce the first one.

## Why the format fits church and school groups

A location quest is a shared walk with a story, not a screen-time activity and not a competition with losers. Every team finishes; the parade at the end is a shared finish, not a podium. The four comfort levels mean the same quest fits a mixed-age group — Cozy for the younger players, a braver level for the older ones — without splitting the group into different games. And the whole thing runs on phones the players already carry: no app to install, no accounts, no hardware to buy.
