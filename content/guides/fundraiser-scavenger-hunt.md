## How the fundraiser model works

Run a ZQM Labs Community kit as a fundraiser. The published split is simple: **families pay $15, and your community keeps $5 of every sale**. You set the price above $15 if you like — the split is fixed, the upside is yours.

The kit itself is the [Community tier — $199 flat](/tiers/), for unlimited families. Fundraising arrangements are available for qualifying events; **coordinate the current terms with the Lab before promoting a fundraiser**, so the split, the settlement, and your event window are all agreed in writing first.

## A worked example

Say 40 families take part at $15 each:

- **Collected:** 40 × $15 = **$600**
- **Your community keeps:** 40 × $5 = **$200**
- **The remaining $10 per family** is the Lab's share of each sale.

By the numbers, 40 families is also the point where your community's $200 share covers the $199 Community kit. Whether your fundraiser settles exactly that way — and whether the kit price is separate from the per-family split — is part of the coordination call: **confirm it with the Lab before you promote anything**. Do not advertise a payout figure the Lab has not agreed for your event.

## Promotion checklist for a PTA or HOA

- **Agree terms first.** Confirm the split, the settlement, your event window, and who handles money — in writing, with the Lab — before a single flyer goes out.
- **Pick your window.** A weekend window works best: families play when it suits them, and a multi-day window survives a rain shower.
- **Name the cause.** "Help fund the new playground" raises more than "fundraiser." Say exactly what the money is for.
- **Use the channels you already have.** The PTA newsletter, the HOA Facebook group, the school pickup line, the neighborhood email list — one message in each beats a poster nobody sees.
- **Show, don't describe.** Link the [free browser demo](/games/hunt-for-wisps/play/?demo=1) so families can try a quest before they pay. A family that has played the demo buys faster than one that has only read a flyer.
- **Set a sign-up deadline.** "Sign up by Friday" creates the decision; an open-ended invitation does not.
- **Plan the finish.** A shared finish — a park, a school field, a community room — turns 40 separate family walks into one event people remember and photograph.
- **Thank people in public.** A short thank-you note with a photo after the event is what gets you invited back next year.

## Pitfalls to plan around

- **Permissions.** A route through a park, a school ground, or a shared HOA space needs the owner's permission. Confirm it before you print signs — not the morning of the event.
- **Weather.** Florida showers pass, and a multi-day window lets families pick a dry moment. If a real storm wipes out the weekend, the Lab will extend a window — ask, don't assume.
- **Accessibility.** Not every family walks the same way. Check your route for step-free access, shade, seating, and distance before you announce it, and say plainly what the route asks of players.
- **Supervision.** Young teams need an adult with them. Say so in your sign-up, and size your volunteer plan around it.
- **Overpromising.** Never advertise a route, a date, a payout, or a feature the Lab has not confirmed for your event. "By coordination" is a promise you can keep; "available now" is one you might not.
- **Money handling.** Agree in advance who collects payments, how the $5-per-family share is settled, and when. Keep it in writing.
