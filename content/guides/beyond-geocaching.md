## What geocaching is — and who it fits best

Geocaching is the best-known GPS treasure hunt in the world: players use an app to find hidden containers left by other players, all over the world. Find the cache, sign the log, move on. The hunt itself is the game, and the community that hides the caches is a large part of it.

It fits some players perfectly:

- **Solo players** who like exploring on their own schedule, one cache at a time.
- **Collectors** who enjoy the find itself — the logbook, the trackables, the count.
- **Families with older kids** who like a walk with a purpose and a puzzle at the end of it.

It is not built for a group event. One person with a phone finds one container; a team of eight standing around one cache is not a game, it is a queue. That is the gap a group treasure hunt fills.

## What a group treasure hunt is

A location quest is a story-driven scavenger hunt built for a group: a team follows a trail of six stops, solves a puzzle at each one, and finishes together. A ZQM Labs quest adds four comfort levels so the same quest fits children and adults, and one phone per team — the phone is the map, the puzzle book, and the scorekeeper.

- **Players:** teams of four to eight, one phone per team. Built for groups, not solo hunters.
- **Where:** your venue or a coordinated local setting — not a fixed route someone else chose.
- **Story:** yes, written for the event. A wandering wisp who lost her lights; a farmer whose pumpkins escaped; a winter festival with a hidden secret.
- **Device:** the browser. No app to install, no account to make.
- **Cost:** one flat fee — Family Night $49 (or $25 with code FAMILY25 at checkout), Community $199, Office Team $349, Office Event $499. No per-player charges.

## Who should stick with geocaching

Say it plainly: if you are a solo player, a container collector, or someone who loves the global hunt and its community, geocaching is your game and nothing here replaces it. A location quest does not give you a worldwide map of caches, and it does not try to. Keep caching.

## Who should try a quest

- **A family that wants one shared event.** One team, one story, one finish — and a comfort level the youngest player can enjoy.
- **A group planning a party or a fundraiser.** Teams play the same route in parallel and finish at the same gathering point. The [fundraiser guide](/guides/fundraiser-scavenger-hunt/) covers the split (families pay $15, your community keeps $5 of every sale).
- **A team that tried a GPS hunt and wanted more story.** If the find was fun but the day felt thin, a quest adds the part that was missing: a reason the stops belong together.
- **A geocacher hosting for non-cachers.** If your group has never cached and will not start now, a quest gives them the treasure-hunt feeling without the app, the learning curve, or the solo format.
