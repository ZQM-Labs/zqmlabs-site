## Games by group type

Parenting blogs list dozens of outdoor games. Most of them are a ball and a vague idea. A game your group will actually finish needs a shape: a start, a set of stops, and an ending everyone reaches together. That is what a location quest is — and here is how it fits each kind of group.

### Family night

One household, one evening, one phone. A six-stop quest around your neighborhood or a local park: the kids solve the puzzles, the adults hold the phone and keep the team moving. The Cozy level is built for young children — no scares, no hard puzzles, just the story.

- **Group size:** one household, four to eight players.
- **Kit:** Family Night, $49 — or $25 with code FAMILY25 at checkout.
- **Setup:** about an hour — print the signs, hand out the team code, start.

### Neighborhood

Several households, one shared event. Teams form by street or by family, play the same route, and finish at the same gathering point — a yard, a park pavilion, a community room. The shared finish is the point: it turns parallel play into a neighborhood event.

- **Group size:** 20 to 60 people across several teams.
- **Kit:** Community, $199.
- **Setup:** about an hour, plus the route and permissions agreed with the Lab beforehand.

### Community and HOA

A fundraiser or a seasonal event for a whole community. Teams pay to play and the community keeps part of each sale — families pay $15, your community keeps $5 of every sale. The [fundraiser guide](/guides/fundraiser-scavenger-hunt/) works the arithmetic and the promotion checklist in full.

- **Group size:** as large as the community — teams of four to eight, as many teams as the route can hold.
- **Kit:** Community, $199, with the fundraiser split.
- **Setup:** about an hour on the day, plus promotion beforehand. The PTA/HOA checklist in the fundraiser guide covers who to tell and when.

### School

A class, a grade, or a whole school on a field-trip day. Teams of four to eight with a teacher or parent per team, a route around the school grounds or a nearby park, and a finish the whole school can see. The [youth-group guide](/guides/youth-group-scavenger-hunt/) covers supervision and group sizes in detail.

- **Group size:** one class to a whole grade, in teams.
- **Kit:** Community, $199, or contact the Lab for a school event.
- **Setup:** about an hour, plus the route and permissions agreed with the Lab and the school beforehand.

## Safety and accessibility planning

Outdoor games fail on safety when nobody planned for it. Plan for these before the day, not on it:

- **One adult per team** for any group with children. The adult holds the phone and keeps the team together.
- **A route everyone can walk.** Tell the Lab about wheelchairs, strollers, and anyone who cannot manage steps or rough ground — the route, stops, and accessibility are agreed for your group and your date, not assumed.
- **A visible finish.** Every team should end at a place with shade, water, and a first-aid kit — a pavilion, a community room, a yard.
- **A contact number.** One organizer's phone number, shared with every team leader, for a lost team or a scraped knee.
- **Permissions.** A route across private land, a school ground, or a park needs permission before the event, not forgiveness after it. The Lab agrees the route and permissions with you beforehand.

## Weather plans

Florida weather is a planning input, not a surprise:

- **Showers:** a multi-day event window lets teams pick a dry moment. Do not cancel on a forecast — cancel on actual weather.
- **Storms:** if a real storm wipes out the weekend, ask the Lab to extend the window. Extensions are a conversation, not a guarantee — ask before the event, not after.
- **Heat:** morning or late-afternoon starts, water at every stop if you can manage it, and shade at the finish. A midday August start with small children is a bad plan.
- **The one plan that fails:** a single fixed date with no flexibility and no indoor fallback. If the date cannot move, have an indoor option ready — the [team-building guide](/guides/team-building-games/) compares indoor options fairly.
