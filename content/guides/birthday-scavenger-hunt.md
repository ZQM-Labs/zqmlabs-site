## Choosing the quest

A birthday scavenger hunt is a location quest with a birthday at the end of it: teams follow a trail of stops, solve a puzzle at each one, and finish together — ideally where the cake is. Start with the [free browser demo](/games/hunt-for-wisps/play/?demo=1) and play it yourself first. It is the same game, in your browser, and ten minutes with the demo answers every question a description cannot: how hard the puzzles are, how the hints work, and whether your birthday player will love it.

The quest is **Family Night — $49** for one household, one quest. Use code **FAMILY25** at checkout for $24 off — play for $25. That is the whole kit: six stops, puzzles, three hints at every stop, printable QR signs, team access codes, a one-page organizer guide, and printable finisher badges. You print the signs yourself (7 pages; card stock recommended).

## Group size and ages

- **One household, up to about 10 players:** Family Night, as published. Split into two teams of four to five, one phone per team, and race — or play as one team and enjoy it slowly.
- **Two or three families together:** still Family Night if it is one household's party; the [Community tier ($199, unlimited families)](/tiers/) is the honest fit once it is genuinely a neighborhood or multi-family event.
- **Younger players (under 10):** play on **Cozy**, the gentlest comfort level. Put an adult on each team — young teams need an adult with them.
- **Mixed ages:** the four comfort levels let the same quest fit the whole party. Let the birthday player pick the level — it is their day.
- **Teenagers and adults:** a braver level (Spooky or After Dark) makes the same six stops a different game. Overdrive is the feral one; save it for a group that wants it.

## Timing

- **Play time:** plan about an hour to ninety minutes for six stops, including the finish.
- **Setup:** about an hour — print the signs, place them, hand out the team codes. Do setup before the guests arrive, not while they are standing around.
- **When to play:** late afternoon works best in Florida — the light is kind and the heat has passed. A morning party works too; midday in summer does not.
- **The finish:** end where the food is. A shared finish — a yard, a park pavilion, a community room — turns separate team walks into one party people remember and photograph.

## Setup in about an hour

1. **Buy the kit.** Family Night is usable on checkout; the kit arrives by email.
2. **Print the signs.** 7 pages; card stock recommended. Print the finisher badges too.
3. **Place the signs.** Six stops, in your yard, on your block, or in a park you have permission for. Get permission first — a park or a shared space needs its owner's permission before you print signs.
4. **Hand out team codes.** One phone per team. Show the first team how the demo works, then let them go.
5. **Be the finish.** Stand at the last stop with the cake. That is the whole job.

## Weather and accessibility

- **Weather:** Florida showers pass. Have a rain date in mind before you announce the first one, and keep the signs somewhere dry until play starts — wet paper signs are the most common birthday failure.
- **Accessibility:** check your route for step-free access, shade, seating, and distance before you announce it, and say plainly what the route asks of players. A yard or a flat park beats a hilly route for a mixed-age party.
- **Supervision:** young teams need an adult with them. Say so on the invitation, and size your adult plan around it.
